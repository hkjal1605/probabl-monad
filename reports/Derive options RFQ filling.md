# Derive's 30-minute RFQ timer marks a deadline

Your instant fills and the video's half-hour wait happen on two different venues inside Derive. **Single-leg options orders placed from the chain ticket in Book or Auto mode go to Derive's central limit order book**, where market, IOC and FOK orders cross resting market-maker orders immediately. RFQ is a separate lane. An order goes there when you build a multi-leg trade (Auto mode switches "based on number of legs"), open the Strategy Builder, or set Execution to RFQ. The video that matches your description is almost certainly Derive's December 2025 maker-side clip. It shows a board of other users' RFQs, with the selected request counting down **"Expires: 29m 36s"**. That clock is the RFQ's **default 30-minute validity window**, the latest moment a quote can still be accepted. It is not an auction length. Nothing on Derive fills automatically when the timer runs out. Makers with an RFQ-maker flag send signed quotes (since December 2025 anyone, retail included, can ask Derive to be whitelisted for the flag). The taker picks one and signs `execute_quote`, and can do that seconds after the first quote arrives. Derive designs for, and pays for, responses within seconds: its maker scoring gives the top time score to quotes sent **within 2 seconds**. A long wait therefore means no automated maker wanted that structure, size or underlying. This usually happens with odd strikes, alt coins such as HYPE, or large packages, and many such RFQs simply expire. Derive's window is also long next to its peers. Deribit, Paradigm, Bybit and OKX give RFQs 2 to 10 minutes. The "wait for the deadline, then the best bid wins" pattern belongs to scheduled on-chain auctions such as Thetanuts V4 and DeFi option-vault rolls, not to block RFQs where a trader asks for a price.

## Instant fills come from the order book, which RFQ never touches

Derive runs two venues, and each signs trades through its own module. Orders use `private/order`, "a signed action against the `TRADE_MODULE`". A `market` order "crosses the book immediately; any unfilled portion is cancelled rather than left resting", and a `limit_price` is **required even for market orders**, as a worst-acceptable price bound. Market, IOC and FOK orders "never leave a resting order in the book" ([Derive docs: Orderbook Trading](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/order-types)). RFQ quotes are signed under a different contract, `RFQ_MODULE 0x9371352CCef6f5b36EfDFE90942fFE622Ab77F1D` ([Derive docs: Action signing](https://docs.derive.xyz/authentication/action-signing)), and each one is tied to a specific `rfq_id`. A book order therefore never trades against an RFQ quote. The docs describe **no automatic routing between the two venues, no size above which RFQ is required, and no minimum RFQ size**. The only links between them are informational: the RFQ object carries orderbook-implied `bid_total_cost` and `ask_total_cost`, and best-quote responses include an `orderbook_total_cost` ([Derive docs: RFQ Trading](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq); [v2 Rfq Get Best Quote](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-rfq-get-best-quote)). There is also an opt-in taker-protection check, covered below.

The app decides where your order goes. Derive's May 2026 tutorial shows that the ETH options ticket has an **Execution** menu with three settings. **Auto** is "Auto-switch between orderbook and RFQ based on number of legs". **Book** is "Always use orderbook". **RFQ** is "Always use RFQ", and turns the ticket into a Submit RFQ form ([YouTube: How to use RFQ directly from book](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OsZDxd3FU-M)). The Strategy Builder always goes through RFQ: "Select strategy. The RFQ panel will automatically open… Click submit RFQ" ([YouTube: How to use strategy builder](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HYvlqKly9bg)). The switch depends on the number of legs, not on size. So a single-leg order goes to the book unless you send it to RFQ yourself. Derive steers larger trades toward RFQ through its marketing ("For larger trades, use RFQ to get direct quotes from MMs" ([X @DeriveXYZ, 17 Apr 2026](https://x.com/DeriveXYZ/status/2044980765663064543))), but that is advice, not a rule the app enforces. No source says whether Auto or Book is the app's default. The demo had Book checked.

Book fills are instant because Derive pays makers to keep the book quoted. Its Institutional Trading Rewards program gives **400k DRV per 28-day epoch to the options order book**, against 200k DRV for options RFQ, plus up to **$500,000 USDC in rebates** per epoch ([Derive docs: Institutional Trading Rewards](https://docs.derive.xyz/integrators/programs/institutional-trading-rewards)). FalconX has quoted "both the central limit orderbook and RFQ" since October 2025, with a focus on short-dated options ([Derive Insights: FalconX](https://insights.derive.xyz/falconx-brings-institutional-liquidity-onchain/)). Derive also says the book has gaps: "Market makers do not sit on every strike of every expiry all day long. They quote when someone asks" ([YouTube: How to use the RFQ on Derive.xyz](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5Kkn5-ZXYSQ)). An order on a liquid ETH or BTC expiry that crosses the spread fills at once against those standing quotes. An order on a strike with an empty book has nothing to cross. Only then does RFQ come into play. Waiting is also not unique to RFQ: a GTC limit order that does not cross rests on the book until someone takes it.

## The video's countdown is a deadline on a board only approved makers see

The clip that fits "anyone can see the request and submit quotes" is **"Anyone Can Be a Market Maker Now On Derive.XYZ"**, posted 8 December 2025 and 61 seconds long. Its description reads "We've opened our RFQ quoting system to everyone… If you can price options, you can quote… DM us on Discord to get whitelisted." In its frames, the RFQ page lists requests from many wallets, with statuses Open, Filled, Expired and Cancelled. The selected request is a **+1 HYPE $50 Call, quantity 100**, reading "Open · Expires: 29m 36s" and then 29m 32s, beside a price field with "Use Mark" and a **Send Quote** button. Captions urge viewers to "Be the first to quote new RFQs". A "Request Access" screen tells users who are not set up as makers to contact support ([YouTube 4QyQ-gaCRl0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4QyQ-gaCRl0)). The CTO's companion post repeats the pitch to retail and professional desks ([Derive Insights: Anyone Can Be a Market Maker](https://insights.derive.xyz/anyone-can-be-a-market-maker-now-on-derive-xyz/)). A countdown at 29m 36s means the RFQ was about 24 seconds old when it was recorded. It shows how long the request stays quotable, not how long it took to fill. The docs give the source of the number: "An RFQ is quotable only for a bounded window after creation (`valid_until`). The default window is 30 minutes", and the exact window "is deployment-specific" ([Derive docs: RFQ Trading](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq)). Derive's own taker tutorial (July 2026) promises the opposite of a wait: "get competitive quotes from market makers **in seconds**". In that demo, a single-leg ETH call RFQ came back about 2% off the book's limit price ([YouTube 5Kkn5-ZXYSQ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5Kkn5-ZXYSQ)). None of the official Derive videos checked shows a taker waiting 30 minutes and then filling. If you saw that sequence, it came from a community video or a livestream, or it showed an RFQ that sat because no maker answered.

"Anyone can see it" is true only among approved makers. A taker who leaves `counterparties` empty opens the RFQ "to all makers". A list of wallet addresses sends it privately to those wallets only ([Derive docs: RFQ Trading](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq)). The v2 docs spell out that "all market makers who are approved as RFQ makers will be notified" ([v2 Send Rfq](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-send-rfq)). Quoting requires the account flag `is_rfq_maker`, and without it a quote fails with error `14027 unauthorized_as_rfq_maker` ([Derive docs: Error codes](https://docs.derive.xyz/error-codes)). **No unauthenticated RFQ feed exists.** Every RFQ channel is private, and the only `public/*` RFQ methods are two signature-debugging helpers ([Derive docs index](https://docs.derive.xyz/llms.txt)). Neither side is anonymous. A maker browsing RFQs sees the taker's `wallet` and `subaccount_id`, the legs, `partial_fill_step`, `valid_until` and fill status. The maker also sees two reputation scores: a lifetime taker `fill_rate` and a decayed `recent_fill_rate` ([Derive API: poll_rfqs](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatepoll_rfqs)). The taker in turn sees "the maker's wallet" on every quote ([Derive API: poll_quotes](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatepoll_quotes)). Makers never see the taker's cost limits or the counterparty list. v2 had an optional `preferred_direction` field that let takers disclose their side, and that field is absent from the v3 schema ([v3 openapi.json](https://docs.derive.xyz/openapi.json)). Current RFQs therefore read as two-way requests, though no migration note says so.

## Nothing fills at the bell: the taker signs the quote it picks

**An RFQ is an unsigned intent, and every fill is a deliberate signature by the taker.** The taker sends `private/send_rfq` with one or more legs. Each leg is `{instrument_name, amount, direction}`, where direction is the taker's side. Multi-leg packages fill "all-or-nothing at a single `total_cost`". The request "does not move funds". Approved makers reply with **EIP-712-signed** `private/send_quote` messages, which price every leg and must match the RFQ's legs exactly. Makers can update a quote with `replace_quote`, withdraw it with `cancel_quote`, or opt it into market-maker protection. The taker collects quotes with `poll_quotes` or the `{subaccount_id}.best.quotes` channel, and can run `rfq_get_best_quote` as a dry run. That call returns the best quote, the estimated cost against `orderbook_total_cost`, fees, the margin impact and an `is_valid` flag. Execution is the taker's signed `private/execute_quote` for a chosen `quote_id`. "The engine re-reads the RFQ and maker quote and settles the block atomically" ([Derive docs: RFQ Trading](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq); [Derive API: rfq_get_best_quote](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privaterfq_get_best_quote)). Three details matter for the "best quote fills" impression. First, **`execute_quote` "fills any valid quote"**, not just the best one. Second, the optional `max_total_cost` and `min_total_cost` filter only the best-quote helper and channel: "They do not limit execution", and "Makers do not see either limit" ([Derive docs: RFQ Trading](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq)). Third, the check that rejects a quote when the order book would fill the same legs more cheaply is **off by default** (`enable_taker_protection`) ([Derive API: execute_quote](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privateexecute_quote); [Derive docs: Price Banding](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/price-banding)). In the app, buy and sell quotes "appear side by side" and the trader selects one ([Derive Insights: Derive's New RFQ Experience](https://insights.derive.xyz/derives-new-rfq-experience/)). A "best quote filled" moment in any video is therefore the taker clicking. The docs never define how "best" is ranked, including tie-breaks.

The lifetimes changed sharply between the v2 API (Derive Chain era) and today's v3 API. v3 merges the exchange and chain into one zero-knowledge application that settles on Ethereum L1 ([Derive docs: Introduction](https://docs.derive.xyz/getting-started/introduction)).

| Timer | v3 (current) | v2 (Derive Chain era) |
|---|---|---|
| RFQ quotable window (`valid_until`) | 30 minutes by default, "deployment-specific" | Not documented |
| Maker quote and taker execute signature expiry | At least RFQ creation + 31 min, at most now + 1 day | At least now + 310 s; the quote counts as expired once 300 s remain |
| Shortest life a maker can give a quote by signing | The rest of the RFQ's window (only cancelling ends it sooner) | About 10 s (docs examples used now + 350 s, about 50 s) |
| RFQ nonce window | ± 1 hour, need not increase | Not compared |
| Order-book orders, for comparison | 10 s to 120 days (MMP orders 15 min) | Not compared |

Sources: [Derive docs: Action signing](https://docs.derive.xyz/authentication/action-signing); [Derive docs: Breaking changes](https://docs.derive.xyz/migrating/breaking-changes); [v2 Send Quote](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-send-quote); [v2 RFQ Quoting and Execution guide](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/rfq-quoting-and-execution).

**Under v3, a maker's quote stays live until the RFQ closes unless the maker pulls it.** The rule `rfq_creation + 31 min ≤ signature_expiry_sec` stops makers from signing short-lived quotes. They must manage staleness actively, through `cancel_quote` and `replace_quote`, market-maker protection (MMP) freezes, and cancel-on-disconnect, which cancels RFQ quotes when the WebSocket drops ([Derive docs: Cancel on Disconnect](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/cancel-on-disconnect); [Derive docs: Market Maker Protection](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/market-maker-protection)). Several guardrails limit the maker's exposure. Price banding rejects maker quotes outside a spot-shock range: 1.5% for ETH and BTC, 3% for HYPE, 10% for some alts, with no vol shock for options. The rejection is error `11107 quote_maker_cost_too_high` ([Derive docs: Price Banding](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/price-banding)). Derive's RFQ and Block Trading Rules (updated 9 May 2026) also let it cancel or adjust executions that are "materially disconnected" from the market ([Derive: RFQ and Block Trading Rules](https://www.derive.xyz/rfq-and-block-trading-rules)). There is no last look: the maker's quote is already signed, so the taker's execution settles atomically. When the window ends, the status becomes `expired`, and any further quote or execution fails with `11104 quote_rfq_not_open` ([Derive docs: Error codes](https://docs.derive.xyz/error-codes)).

Partial fills explain why some RFQs stay open after trading. A taker can fill in multiples of `partial_fill_step` (default 1). Every partial fill must be in the same direction (error `11109`). A partially filled RFQ stays `open` and keeps appearing to makers ([Derive docs: Changelog](https://docs.derive.xyz/changelog); [Derive docs: Error codes](https://docs.derive.xyz/error-codes)). A large request can therefore take one maker's size quickly and then sit while it gathers more quotes. Settlement is one atomic transfer batch. The v2 contract required exactly two signed actions, verified that the taker's signed hash matched the maker's legs, then moved every leg, net cash and both fees together ([derivexyz v2-matching RfqModule.sol](https://github.com/derivexyz/v2-matching/blob/master/src/modules/RfqModule.sol)). In v3, an executed quote reports a `batch_status` that runs from batching through ZK proving to settled ([Derive API: send_quote](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatesend_quote)). The fees favour packages. RFQ trades charge "the taker notional fee rate on both counterparties, plus a base fee on the taker side". The cheapest leg group gets 100% off and the second and third cheapest get 50% off, so a two-leg spread pays nothing on its second leg ([Derive docs: Trading Fees](https://docs.derive.xyz/integrators/trading/trading-fees)). On a single-leg RFQ, the maker pays the 0.03% taker rate instead of the book's 0.01% maker rate, unless its institutional tier discount covers the difference. Single-leg RFQ is a weaker deal for makers than resting on the book.

## Who answers, and how fast, decides the wait

Two things decide whether an order fills instantly. **First, the route.** The Execution setting and the number of legs decide between the book and RFQ, as above. **Second, if the order goes to RFQ, whether a maker wants to price it.** The rules impose no delay: the docs describe no grace period and no minimum exposure time. Derive designs for speed. Its RFQ maker score gives a time score of **2 for a first quote within 2 seconds, 1 within 30 seconds, and 0.5 at 5 minutes or more**. It scores only RFQs that filled, but it scores every maker that quoted on them, with a 1.2× boost for the maker that filled and 0.5× for the others. Price competitiveness is measured against the taker's Max Cost, a number makers cannot see ([Derive docs: Institutional Trading Rewards](https://docs.derive.xyz/integrators/programs/institutional-trading-rewards)). An October 14 read of the same page recorded minute-scale buckets instead (5 minutes or less, 15 minutes or less, over 15 minutes). Either Derive tightened the scoring in the last few weeks or one of the two reads is wrong. Under either version, a quote that arrives minutes late is still accepted and still scores.

Derive's bench of makers is thin and subsidised, so a request that no automated desk wants can go unanswered. FalconX is the only market maker publicly named. Derive's own April 2026 blog names it as its partner market maker ([Derive blog: market makers in crypto options](https://www.derive.xyz/blog/how-do-market-makers-participate-in-crypto-options/)). Oak Research found that DRV trading rewards were **paused at the end of September 2026** and concluded that "the market makers are here, but they're being paid to show up" ([Oak Research](https://oakresearch.io/en/reports/protocols/derive-next-hyperliquid-liquidity-real-moat)). TechFlow cites Alea Research's finding that equivalent risk costs **about 40% more on Derive than on Deribit**. It also puts 30-day on-chain options notional at **$5.49B, about 6.9% of Deribit's $79.8B** ([TechFlow / Foresight News, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/34541)). Opening quoting to retail in December 2025 added makers who price from the board by hand, and they need minutes, not seconds. The board in the maker video shows many RFQs ending Expired or Cancelled, and its captions note that "HYPE options are seeing nonstop taker flow" ([YouTube 4QyQ-gaCRl0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4QyQ-gaCRl0)). Derive publishes no response-time, fill-rate or maker-count statistics. The pattern in the table below is therefore inferred from the mechanics, not measured.

| What you place | Where it goes | When it fills | What sets the timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-leg market, IOC or FOK order (Book or Auto) | Order book | Immediately, or the rest is cancelled | Resting depth within your limit price |
| Single-leg GTC limit order that does not cross the spread | Rests on the order book | When someone takes it, or never | Your price against the market |
| Single-leg RFQ on a liquid ETH or BTC expiry | RFQ, sent to all approved makers | Seconds, once you click a quote | Automated makers' response, usually instant |
| Multi-leg package (Auto mode, Strategy Builder) | RFQ | Seconds to minutes | Whether a maker will price the whole package at once |
| Alt underlying, off-grid strike, or large size | RFQ | Minutes, partially, or never (expires at about 30 min) | Manual makers on the board; partial-fill steps |
| Any RFQ with a tight `max_total_cost` | RFQ | No "best quote" shown until a quote clears your hidden limit | Your own limit, which makers cannot see |

The taker side can create waits too. A `max_total_cost` that no quote meets suppresses the best-quote display even when quotes exist. A quote whose margin check fails comes back with `is_valid` false ([Derive API: rfq_get_best_quote](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privaterfq_get_best_quote)). Some structures cannot be sent at all: legs that span two currencies are rejected (`14028`), and so are counterparties in a different risk universe ([Derive docs: Managers & Risk Universes](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/managers-and-risk-universes)). The waiting flow is therefore not reserved by rule for large or multi-leg orders. It is what any RFQ looks like when nobody fast is interested, and that happens most often with large, multi-leg, illiquid-strike and alt-coin requests.

## Derive's half-hour window runs three to six times longer than peers'

On centralised venues, block RFQs live for minutes, quotes are expected within seconds, and the taker crosses as soon as a price is acceptable. **Deribit Block RFQ** (launched 6 March 2025) shows the taker one best bid and one best ask "a few seconds after the RFQ is submitted". The RFQ expires if the taker has not traded within **5 minutes**, after a 5-second grace period during which quotes are hidden ([Deribit Insights](https://insights.deribit.com/education/new-deribit-block-rfq-feature-launches/); [Deribit Block RFQ API walkthrough](https://docs.deribit.com/articles/block-rfq-api-walkthrough)). Deribit also combines smaller quotes from several makers into one full-size quote. It requires block-sized trades of 25 BTC or 250 ETH of options ([Paradigm help: Deribit venue](https://www.paradigm.co/help/products/derivatives/venues/deribit)), and allows an anonymous taker only when at least 5 makers are targeted ([Deribit API: create_block_rfq](https://docs.deribit.com/api-reference/block-rfq/private-create_block_rfq)). **Paradigm** expires RFQs "which do not trade after 5 minutes from creation", lets only the creator see makers' orders, and has no partial fills ([Paradigm docs: RFQ](https://docs.paradigm.co/unified-markets/rfq-request-for-quotes)). **Bybit** quotes usually arrive "within seconds". The RFQ expires if no quote comes within 10 minutes, and the taker gets **1 minute to accept**, during which the maker may withdraw if the market moves ([Bybit Help: FAQ, Bybit RFQ](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/FAQ-Bybit-RFQ)). **OKX** expires all-option RFQs after 10 minutes and others after 2, and its maker quotes carry their own `expiresIn` of 10 to 120 seconds ([OKX API v5 docs](https://www.okx.com/docs-v5/en/)). Against this, Derive's design stands out in five ways. Its window is 30 minutes. Its quotes must stand for that whole window. Requests are broadcast to every approved maker by default. It sets no minimum size. And it lets retail traders act as makers.

The flow you described, in which a request sits publicly, makers bid, and the best quote fills at the deadline, matches a different family: **timed auctions run by the seller or issuer**. **Thetanuts V4** on Base is the closest match. Its RFQs are sealed-bid: makers submit encrypted offers before a deadline the user sets (the SDK examples use 6, 30 and 60 minutes), then reveal them, the best offer wins, and "Anyone calls settleQuotation()". The requester can also settle early, and one real RFQ settled 3 minutes before its deadline ([Thetanuts SDK](https://github.com/Thetanuts-Finance/thetanuts-sdk); [Thetanuts SDK docs/rfq](https://github.com/Thetanuts-Finance/thetanuts-sdk/tree/main/docs/rfq)). DeFi option vaults used the same shape. Ribbon ran weekly auctions through Gnosis and later through Paradigm's blind, all-or-nothing auctions ([Ribbon docs: Auctions](https://docs.ribbon.finance/theta-vault/theta-vault/auctions)). Opyn's Crab strategy ran fixed-schedule bid windows of about 10 minutes ([Opyn: Participating in the squeeth crab auction](https://medium.com/opyn/participating-in-the-squeeth-crab-auction-b75a1defd8d6)). These designs suit sellers whose timing is fixed, such as a vault that must roll every week. Sealed bids protect makers from being picked off during the window, a protection Derive's open, long-lived quotes do not offer.

Traditional options markets run on even shorter clocks. Cboe's AIM price-improvement auction lasts at least 100 ms, and its maximum was cut from 3 seconds to **1 second** in a rule change approved on 10 December 2025 ([Cboe SR-CBOE-2025-095](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/regulation/rule_filings/approved/2025/SR-CBOE-2025-095.pdf)). CME's R-Cross requires the cross order to follow the options RFQ within **15 to 30 seconds**, and it trades against better resting book orders first ([CME Market Regulation Advisory RA1718-5](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rule120517cmedcm004.pdf)). Swap dealers answering RFQs on swap execution facilities send firm quotes "with a clock that counts down (generally 30 seconds)" ([Riggs, Onur, Reiffen & Zhu, JFE 2020](https://www.mit.edu/~zhuh/RiggsOnurReiffenZhu_SEF_JFE.pdf)). Eurex is the instructive contrast. It broadcasts derivatives RfQs to the whole market, but accepts a user's RfQ only when the book is wide or thin. It also **generates an RfQ automatically when a market order fails to match** ([Deutsche Börse T7 Release 15.0 Functional Reference](https://www.cashmarket.deutsche-boerse.com/resource/blob/5409264/023659e230f0c441883c6ad093434a91/data/T7_Release_15.0_-_Functional_Reference-Part_1_Version_2.pdf)). Derive does neither: it accepts RFQs for any instrument at any time, and a failed book order is simply cancelled.

| Venue | Who sees the request | RFQ lifetime | Quote life | How it fills | Minimum size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Derive v3 | All approved RFQ makers, or named wallets | 30 min default | Until the RFQ ends + 60 s, unless cancelled | Taker signs any quote; partial-fill steps | None stated |
| Deribit Block RFQ | All makers or a chosen list (anonymous needs ≥ 5) | 5 min, 5 s grace | Optional maker-set expiry | Taker crosses the combined best bid or ask | 25 BTC / 250 ETH options |
| Paradigm DRFQ | Dealers the taker picks; only the taker sees quotes | 5 min | Not stated | Taker crosses with a limit order; no partials | The venue's block minimum |
| Bybit RFQ | Up to 30 chosen liquidity providers | Up to 10 min to get a quote | 1 min to accept | Taker accepts; all or none | $10k notional |

## Conclusion

On Derive, a long wait carries information. It is not a feature of the mechanism. An RFQ that sits for minutes is telling you that no automated maker will price that package at a level you would accept. Leaving it open mostly invites late manual quotes from the retail makers Derive brought in during December 2025. For fast fills, keep single legs on the book in Book or Auto mode on liquid ETH and BTC expiries. Use RFQ for packages, where the fee discounts and atomic settlement pay off, and expect answers in seconds on liquid structures. When you do use RFQ, three settings protect you. Set `max_total_cost` so the best-quote view hides bad prices. Compare the quote with `orderbook_total_cost`. Turn on `enable_taker_protection`, which is off by default. If nothing competitive arrives within a minute or two, the full 30 minutes rarely changes the outcome.

The design choice behind the wait has a cost. Derive v3 made every quote stand for the full window and put the taker's fill rate in front of makers. That gives the taker a long free option and shifts the defence onto the makers: cancelling, MMP and fill-rate screening. Makers who carry that option for up to 30 minutes, with rewards now paused and few competitors, have reason to quote wider. That fits Alea's finding that equivalent risk costs about 40% more than on Deribit, though no data isolates this cause. Venues that need firm prices from makers cut both clocks short instead. Deribit and Paradigm give the RFQ minutes; OKX and Bybit give quotes seconds. A timed "best bid wins" auction is worth its delay only when the seller cannot choose when to trade and the bids are sealed. That is the setting Thetanuts and the DeFi vaults built for, not the one a trader asking for a price is in.
