# Curve the conditional gap, not the claims

Probabl cannot bootstrap its markets with a pump.fun bonding curve, but it can bootstrap them with a curve on the one number its markets actually have to discover. pump.fun gets liquidity for free because it mints its inventory from nothing into a closed supply with no outside price. Every Probabl claim must instead be backed by a real share or dollar, anyone can split and merge claims, and spot stock plus Polymarket pin the price, so a launch curve set below fair value is simply drained by arbitrage: opened at half of fair value it hands over about 9% of its inventory value, at a tenth about 47%. Once the stock price s comes from Chainlink and the event probability p from Polymarket, each market has a single unknown, the conditional gap δ = (Y − N)/s. An oracle-anchored δ-curve that quotes both branches at trade time, raising the YES price and lowering the NO price as YES demand grows and the reverse for NO, discovers that gap while holding p·Y + (1−p)·N = s by construction. In simulation at equal depth it lost about **$45 per 30-day event** to perfectly informed traders against **$2,300** for a MetaDAO-style split-seeded constant-product pair, and it needs roughly **$100k of stock and USDG per stock**, shared by up to eight events, for ≤1% all-in slippage on a $10k trade, against $2.86M per market for the full-range pair. The recommendation is to keep the order books and put this curve behind them as an always-quoting backstop: first as keeper-posted just-in-time quotes with no contract change, then as an on-chain pool beside the immutable exchange. It does not remove the need for capital, which no fully collateralized venue can avoid. It imports Polymarket's p through a trusted signer, it must halt whenever the equity oracle is stale or the market is closed, and the volatility of δ itself, which every loss figure scales with, has never been measured.

## pump.fun's free liquidity rests on minting that Probabl cannot do

pump.fun's curve is a Uniswap-v2 constant product run on virtual reserves of 30 SOL and 1.073B tokens. It sells 793.1M of a 1B supply, then seeds a PumpSwap pool with the remainder and the proceeds and burns the LP tokens ([pump-public-docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/main/docs/PUMP_PROGRAM_README.md)). Worked through, the curve raises about **85 SOL**, its price rises **14.7×** from the first token to the last, and the average buyer pays about **26% of the graduation price**. Fewer than 2% of launches ever graduate ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/343742/pump-fun-rapid-decline-graduating-tokens-memecoin-frenzy-fizzles-out)). The design needs no LP capital for four stacked reasons: the token is minted free, the virtual reserves set price and depth without money, buyers' payments are recycled into the post-graduation pool, and price rises monotonically with supply sold. The virtual reserves are safe only under a **closed-supply condition**. Every token outside the curve was bought from it, so sellers can never withdraw more SOL than buyers put in, and the 30 virtual SOL never pays out. Later buyers pay for the depth through the convex price path, and nobody can lose against fair value because no fair value exists.

Probabl breaks every one of those conditions. Each claim is backed one-for-one: `split` turns one stock token into one YES and one NO claim, and the exchange keeps backing equal to the sum of YES claims and to the sum of NO claims ([ConditionalStocks.sol](../contracts/src/ConditionalStocks.sol), [cross-chain-order-support.md](../docs/cross-chain-order-support.md)). Supply is open, since anyone holding stock can split it and sell into a curve's bid. Prices are anchored too. Split and merge alone force min(Y, N) ≤ s ≤ max(Y, N), and hedging the leftover branch-dollar claims on Polymarket tightens that to p·Y + (1−p)·N ≈ s. A simulated virtual-reserve curve opened at half of a fair YES price of 103 (s = 100, p = 0.4) sells 30% of its claims to arbitrageurs and gives away **9,473 YES-USD**. Opened at a tenth of fair value, it sells 69% and gives away **48,813 YES-USD** ([S1 simulation](../research_notes/Conditional%20market%20liquidity%20bootstrapping/mechsim/s1_out.txt)). With the NO book at 98, the arbitrage is riskless: pay 98 dollars, merge, sell the stock, and keep $21.20 per share. Seeding the curve is no better. Putting 1,000 YES-stock claims on it means splitting 1,000 shares, which leaves 1,000 NO-stock claims worth about **$58,800** as an unhedged event bet unless a NO curve sells them, and at that point the design has become a split-seeded pool pair. There is nothing to graduate, and launch sniping becomes pure extraction from whoever seeded the curve. The market has drawn the same conclusion. pump.fun's Custom Pairs and Robinhood Chain's Pons launchpad use tokenized NVDA, TSLA and SPY as the *quote* asset for freely minted memecoins, never as curve inventory ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/pump-fun-lets-creators-launch-coins-priced-in-tokenized-stocks), [Bitquery Pons docs](https://docs.bitquery.io/docs/blockchain/robinhood/pons-api/)).

The house-counterparty products fail for the same root reason. Rollbit's futures are synthetic wagers against the company's balance sheet, with open positions capped at $1M per user and a haircut on winners' profit ([Rollbit blog](https://blog.rollbit.com/1000x-leverage-crypto-futures/)). A 2023 analysis put that haircut at an effective 10–30% edge on high-leverage flow ([Delphi Digital](https://members.delphidigital.io/feed/how-has-rollbit-done-over-72b-in-futures-volume-last-month)). Papertrade, which opened trading on **October 10, 2026**, goes further. Its LP starts at **$0** and accepts no deposits, and winners the pool cannot pay join a FIFO queue funded by later losses. Losers are minted 100 PAPER per dollar lost, and prices come from Hyperliquid's best-bid-offer mid with no deviation breaker ([Papertrade docs](https://docs.papertrade.xyz/)). A pre-launch simulation shows where the money comes from: liquidations at up to 1000×. In it, 88.1% of BTC trades paid the LP and traders lost 73 cents per dollar of margin ([Sumcap](https://www.sumcap.xyz/blog/the-house-always-wins)). An unconfirmed oracle-manipulation allegation surfaced on launch day ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/papertrade-oracle-manipulation-allegation-pool/)). None of this carries over to a fully collateralized spot venue. A winner's stock claim has to be delivered, not queued. Probabl has no leverage, so it has no liquidation edge to refill a pool. And loss-mining tokens invite wash farming, the kind of points-driven growth the repo's own strategy note rules out ([future-improvements.md](../docs/future-improvements.md)).

What survives from both families is smaller but real. One deposit can fund both branches, and a curve can serve as a quoting function on real inventory centred on an external anchor. Proceeds can be recycled into standing bids, as Doppler's lower slug and Flaunch's bid wall do ([Doppler.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/whetstoneresearch/doppler/main/docs/Doppler.md), [Flaunch](https://docs.flaunch.gg/features/auto-buybacks.md)). A uniform-price opening auction can replace first-block sniping ([Uniswap blog](https://blog.uniswap.org/continuous-clearing-auctions)). And launch snipe taxes can be re-keyed to oracle staleness instead of time since launch. Those pieces shape the design below.

## Spot and Polymarket leave each market a single unknown

Probabl's design doc already states the decomposition that makes a curve workable. Once the stock price s and event probability p are known, the YES-book price is Y = s·(1 + (1−p)·δ) and the NO-book price is N = s·(1 − p·δ), so the only thing left to discover is the conditional gap δ ([delta-vault.md](../docs/delta-vault.md)). The Δ-vault keeper prices exactly this model off chain. It moves its estimate δ̂ against the vault's YES-minus-NO stock imbalance ([model.ts](../services/vault-keeper/src/model.ts)). This one equation decides how much any liquidity mechanism loses. A pool that learns Y and N on its own pays loss-versus-rebalancing on the stock's full volatility, ½·D·s·σ² per unit time at depth D ([Milionis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06046)). A curve anchored to s and p pays only on the movement of δ, ½·D·s·p(1−p)·σ_δ², plus oracle error. Event news is less dangerous than it looks. If δ stays fixed in dollar terms, a jump in p moves spot by (Y − N)·dp and leaves both branch prices where they were, so a pool that quotes conditional prices is not stale after news. "Price discovery on both legs" therefore has a precise meaning here: discover δ, while the mid always satisfies p·Y + (1−p)·N = s.

The books are thin today because none of the current machinery delivers that discovery at size. A fill happens only when a taker's plan names a resting maker. A taker can name at most **eight**, and each extra maker adds about 17k gas to an 85k single-maker fill ([README](../README.md), [ConditionalStocksBase.sol](../contracts/src/ConditionalStocksBase.sol)). A vault tracks at most 48 orders across up to eight markets ([DeltaVaultBase.sol](../contracts/src/vault/DeltaVaultBase.sol)). The keeper posts about **$2,000 per ladder** in two levels 25 bp apart, behind a half-spread floor of at least 40 bp, so the vault cannot fill a $10k order at all ([config.example.json](../services/vault-keeper/config.example.json), [config.ts](../services/vault-keeper/src/config.ts)). Each testnet market's imbalance cap is **2 shares**, which leaves roughly one share of depth per branch side, and the vault plan still points at the exchange that was replaced on October 5 ([46630-vault-plan.json](../deployments/46630-vault-plan.json)). Both bots also refuse to quote when Polymarket's spread exceeds 5 points, its depth falls under $100, or p leaves [2%, 98%], so long-shot events get no liquidity at all ([reference.ts](../packages/operator/src/reference.ts)). The pricing model is right. What is missing is capital, parameters sized to that capital, and execution at a fresh price at the moment of the trade.

## Only the δ-curve is cheap in both capital and arbitrage loss

The candidates were compared at equal depth: a $10k one-leg trade costs at most 1% all-in, including a 30 bp half-spread, at s = $100. The comparison uses stdlib Python models written for this study. The Monte Carlo runs assume perfectly informed arbitrageurs who trade every minute and noise traders who pay the full spread. The absolute profit levels are therefore indicative, while the ranking is more robust.

| Mechanism | Capital for ≤1% all-in on a $10k trade | Loss to informed flow per 30-day event | External inputs | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pump.fun curve per branch | Full backing, plus an unhedged opposite-leg bet | 9–47% of inventory value at launch | none | Does not fit |
| Papertrade or Rollbit house pool | Not applicable: fully backed claims cannot be queued | Not applicable | external mid | Does not fit |
| Split-seeded constant-product pair, full range (MetaDAO v0.6) | $2.86M per market | −$2,297 | none | Works, trustless, capital-hungry; fallback |
| Same pair, concentrated ±20–30% | $249k–$351k per market; range exited in 26% / 7% of 30-day events | same dollar loss | none | Fragile |
| LMSR in stock units | $248k worst-case subsidy | ≈ $200k expected | none | Wrong numeraire |
| s-anchored curve that learns p ("B") | ≈ $100k per stock | −$121 at δ 5%; a $3.4k one-off surprise loss at δ 20% | s | Small-gap scheduled events only |
| **Oracle-anchored δ-curve ("C")** | **≈ $100k per stock, shared by ≤ 8 events** | **−$45** | s, p | **Recommended backstop** |
| Opening δ-auction | none extra | cuts later discovery cost to $5–$47 | s, p at clearing | Add-on |
| JIT spot routing, or pairing YES and NO buyers | spot stock plus a Polymarket hedge | event P&L sd $52k on balanced flow | spot, Polymarket | Hedging tool only |

Sources: [S2](../research_notes/Conditional%20market%20liquidity%20bootstrapping/mechsim/s2_out.txt), [S3](../research_notes/Conditional%20market%20liquidity%20bootstrapping/mechsim/s3_out.txt), [S4](../research_notes/Conditional%20market%20liquidity%20bootstrapping/mechsim/s4_out.txt) and [S5](../research_notes/Conditional%20market%20liquidity%20bootstrapping/mechsim/s5_out.txt) simulations.

The split-seeded pair is the closest working precedent. MetaDAO's current Futarchy AMM (v0.6, November 2025) takes half of the DAO's spot pool reserves when a proposal launches and splits them into pass and fail constant-product pools. Both pools start at the spot price, and the winning pool's reserves are merged back at finalization ([launch_proposal.rs](https://github.com/metaDAOproject/programs/blob/a8ca9f1d34ee8b135bf8fffe5ba7e8aa4cb10c9f/programs/futarchy/src/instructions/launch_proposal.rs), [finalize_proposal.rs](https://github.com/metaDAOproject/programs/blob/a8ca9f1d34ee8b135bf8fffe5ba7e8aa4cb10c9f/programs/futarchy/src/instructions/finalize_proposal.rs)). MetaDAO got there after on-chain order books "led to liquidity problems" ([Neodyme audit](https://www.neodyme.io/reports/MetaDAO.pdf)). Proposal volume then rose from **$205K in Q3 2025 to $3.6M in Q4 2025**, though more launches arrived over the same period ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/metadao-q4-2025-quarterly-report)). For Probabl, this pair is the only design that needs no oracle at all. It discovers both legs by arbitrage, an LP's payoff at resolution equals an ordinary constant-product position that followed the winning branch's price path, and event news costs it nothing. Its weakness is the stock's ordinary volatility. It lost about **$2,300 per 30-day event** to informed flow in simulation, and about 40% of that came from overnight and weekend gaps, which faster blocks cannot remove. It needs **$2.86M per market** at full range to hit the depth target, or $249k–$351k concentrated in ±20–30%, where a ±20% range is exited in 26% of 30-day events. Seeding both pools at spot, as MetaDAO does, suits MetaDAO's 3% default pass threshold ([lib.rs](https://github.com/metaDAOproject/programs/blob/a8ca9f1d34ee8b135bf8fffe5ba7e8aa4cb10c9f/programs/futarchy/src/lib.rs)). On earnings- or merger-type events, where the gap is larger, it hands the gap to arbitrageurs.

Scoring-rule market makers are the textbook answer to thin prediction markets, because the sponsor's worst-case loss is fixed at b·ln n ([Hanson 2002](https://mason.gmu.edu/~rhanson/mktscore.pdf)). But they price claims that pay $1 in a state, not a share of stock. Run in stock units, trading YES-stock and NO-stock against whole stock, an LMSR takes the event side, and its state has to travel to 0 or 1 by resolution. At equal depth its expected loss is about **$200k per market** against a $248k worst case ([S3](../research_notes/Conditional%20market%20liquidity%20bootstrapping/mechsim/s3_out.txt)). Running it on Y instead would need bucketed, cash-settled claims, which gives up physical delivery. The record on outcome-token AMMs is also poor. In Polymarket's 2020–2022 fixed-product era, impermanent loss exceeded fees in every volume bin and only **26% of LPs** made money. In its order-book era, **69.7% of makers** were profitable, with about $9.3M of rewards paid ([TUM thesis](https://www.cs.cit.tum.de/fileadmin/w00cfj/sebis/_my_direct_uploads/20250903_Parshant_MA_Thesis.pdf)). Paradigm's pm-AMM still loses half of LP wealth by expiry ([Paradigm](https://www.paradigm.xyz/2024/11/pm-amm)). That collapse does not apply inside a Probabl branch book, where both assets share the condition and p cancels out. It is a reason not to list the event leg itself, which Polymarket already prices.

The δ-curve wins because its capital is set by what it must deliver, not by virtual reserves, and because it pays no LVR on the stock. It lost about **$45 per event** to informed flow, roughly **50× less** than the pair, and exactly $0 when δ never moved. A $10k fill needs about $20k of deliverable inventory, and about $100k per stock covers the caps for ≤1% all-in on $10k, shared by up to eight events of that stock ([S3](../research_notes/Conditional%20market%20liquidity%20bootstrapping/mechsim/s3_out.txt), [S4](../research_notes/Conditional%20market%20liquidity%20bootstrapping/mechsim/s4_out.txt)). Its pricing borrows from venues where an oracle supplies only part of the price. DODO's PMM centres an inventory curve on an oracle price ([DODO](https://docs.dodoex.io/en/product/pmm-algorithm/details-about-pmm)), and Synthetix charged a premium proportional to open-interest skew ([SIP-279](https://sips.synthetix.io/sips/sip-279)). Thales and Overtime move bookmaker odds with the pool's own inventory and quote only between 5% and 95% odds ([Thales docs](https://docs.thales.io/thales-sports-markets/singles-market-making.md)). Azuro caps each market's worst-case loss at a fixed reinforcement ([Azuro docs](https://github.com/Azuro-protocol/gem-docs/blob/HEAD/src/app/knowledge-hub/how-azuro-works/components/vAMM/page.mdx)). In the δ-curve, both legs are discovered through one state variable, with impact λ per share:

| Trade of q shares | δ̂ | YES price Y | NO price N |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy YES-stock | + λq | + s(1−p)λq | − s·p·λq |
| Buy NO-stock | − λq | − s(1−p)λq | + s·p·λq |
| Buy both legs equally (that is, buy stock) | unchanged | moves only through the full-set skew | moves only through the full-set skew |

Sells mirror these moves, and the mid keeps satisfying p·Y + (1−p)·N = s. The cost of the design is that it imposes Polymarket's p. A trader who disagrees with Polymarket can express that view only on the order book, which is one reason to keep the book.

Two candidates help without solving the problem. The first is a uniform-price opening auction on δ, cleared against s and p in the clearing block. It aggregates early information and removes launch sniping. With ten informed bidders on a merger-type event, it cuts the error in the starting δ from 15% to 1.3% and the later discovery cost from **$2,009 to $14**, but it adds no continuous depth ([S5](../research_notes/Conditional%20market%20liquidity%20bootstrapping/mechsim/s5_out.txt)). Uniswap's Continuous Clearing Auction shows the pattern working at scale: Aztec raised about $59–61M from roughly 17,000 bidders with no clear sniping. But it sells a fixed supply, while conditional claims are elastic ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/381618/aztec-network-raises-over-60-million-in-eth-with-community-first-token-sale-testing-new-auction-model), [Uniswap blog](https://blog.uniswap.org/aztec-cca)). The second is pairing YES and NO buyers, Polymarket's MINT match, which does not transfer because the collateral here is a share of stock ([ctf-exchange-v2](https://github.com/Polymarket/ctf-exchange-v2)). Even perfectly balanced random flow left an unmatched leg with an unhedged event P&L standard deviation of about **$52k** on $150k/day of flow, and whoever holds that leg is a market maker by another name ([S5](../research_notes/Conditional%20market%20liquidity%20bootstrapping/mechsim/s5_out.txt)). Spot routing is a hedging tool for the vault, not a source of liquidity.

## Oracle freshness, not curve shape, decides whether the backstop makes money

The Monte Carlo model ran 300 paths of a 30-day event with s = $100, σ = 45%, δ = 5% with σ_δ = 5% and one jump, and $150k/day of $3k noise trades. Figures are LP profit in today's dollars, with the 5th percentile in brackets where it matters.

| Scenario | A: split-seeded pair | B: s-anchored, learns p | C: δ-curve |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base, trading halted while the stock market is closed | 4,434 | 6,617 | 6,414 (5,911) |
| Informed flow only | −2,297 | −121 | −45 (−152) |
| High-gap event (δ 20%, start error 5%) | 4,413 | 4,869 (2,066) | 6,317 (5,670) |
| Jumpy gap (σ_δ 50%, three 5% jumps) | 2,570 | 4,760 | 5,105 (3,721) |
| s oracle 5 minutes stale | 4,515 | 2,360 | 1,068 (−2,516) |
| p oracle 5 minutes stale, high-gap event | 4,308 | 4,880 | 2,385 (−4,038) |
| Trading while closed: live p, frozen s | 4,420 | 4,172 | 2,831 (−1,300) |
| Trading while closed, high-gap event | 4,321 | 2,443 | −1,475 (−11,132) |

Source: [S4 simulation](../research_notes/Conditional%20market%20liquidity%20bootstrapping/mechsim/s4_out.txt).

With fresh oracles the δ-curve earns essentially its spread. With a 5-minute-stale stock price its mean falls from $6,414 to **$1,068**, with a 5th percentile of −$2,516, which is worse than the oracle-free pair. Trading through closed hours with a live p and a frozen s halves its profit, and on a high-gap event it turns negative. Errors in p matter only on large gaps. A stale or biased p misprices both legs by δ·dp, which stays inside a 30 bp spread unless δ·dp exceeds 0.3%; on a 20% gap, a 5-minute p lag cuts the mean to $2,385. The rules follow directly. The curve must halt whenever the equity feed is stale or the market is closed, require fresh p on high-gap markets, and stop before any scheduled announcement. A surprise outcome announced while the curve still trades costs the s-anchored variant about $3.4k at δ = 20%, but costs a δ-curve with fresh p nothing ([S5](../research_notes/Conditional%20market%20liquidity%20bootstrapping/mechsim/s5_out.txt)). Chainlink's 24/5 equity Data Streams cover pre-market, post-market and overnight sessions and carry market-status flags ([Chainlink docs](https://docs.chain.link/data-streams/rwa-streams/24-5-us-equities-user-guide)). They have run on Robinhood Chain since its July 1, 2026 mainnet ([Genfinity](https://genfinity.io/2026/07/01/robinhood-chain-mainnet-launch-chainlink-oracle-integration)), so only weekends and exchange holidays need halts. Whether Data Streams equities exist on Base was not confirmed.

The δ-curve turns the liquidity problem into an oracle problem, and the oracle problem into a key-management problem. No production on-chain Polymarket probability feed exists ([delta-vault.md](../docs/delta-vault.md)), so p must come from a Probabl-signed relay. That is the same trust the keeper already holds, bounded by |δ̂|·Δp, the Chainlink band and the caps. History says this is where the money goes. Ostium lost **$23.75M** in six minutes in July 2026 when its price-signing infrastructure was compromised ([The Block](https://theblock.co/post/410122/ostium-post-mortem-exploit)). Drift lost about **$285M** to social-engineered multisig signers in April 2026 ([QuillAudits](https://www.quillaudits.com/blog/hack-analysis/drift-protocol-multisig-exploit)). GMX v1's zero-price-impact pool lost about $565k in 2022 to a trader who repeatedly moved AVAX on other venues and traded against the oracle ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/gmx-dex-suffers-565000-exploit-on-avalanche-avax/)). The pools that survived converged on a common set of defences, and all of them apply here: a fresh pulled price no more than a few seconds old and within 5% of Chainlink ([Veranta docs](https://docs.veranta.xyz/trading/execution-and-pricing/accurate-oracle-execution.md)), skew-based impact instead of flat quotes ([GMX docs](https://docs.gmx.io/docs/trading/fees)), and closing-fee decay or a short settlement delay against latency round trips ([gTrade docs](https://docs.gains.trade/gtrade-leveraged-trading/fees-and-spread), [SIP-279](https://sips.synthetix.io/sips/sip-279)). For Probabl, that means cross-checking p with multiple signers or a second source, and clamping how far an accepted p may move per minute. The pool should halt on jumps of 8 points or more, keep a full-set skew so that stock-equivalent trades never see infinite depth at s ± h, and cap notional per block, because otherwise a $10k trade with 1.4% marginal impact is profitable to sandwich against a 60 bp round trip.

Two Probabl-specific risks need guards of their own. The first is collateral dislocation. Tokenized HIMS on Robinhood Chain reportedly traded at 4.6× its reference until the issuer minted about 6.8% more supply, though this comes from an unverified secondary report ([bit.com](https://www.bit.com/square/robinhood-chain-vs-solana-base-memecoin)). An oracle-anchored curve would then be arbitraged through split and merge against the on-chain token, so the pool should compare the token's on-chain price with the oracle and widen or pause on a breach. The second is manipulation of δ̂. Moving δ by 2.8% costs one $10k one-leg trade, so δ̂ is cheap to nudge but expensive to hold. Anything that reads it, such as the keeper's consistency breaker or a future Δ data feed, should use a lagging TWAP that caps each update's move, as MetaDAO's does ([MetaDAO price-oracle.md](https://github.com/metaDAOproject/programs/blob/a8ca9f1d34ee8b135bf8fffe5ba7e8aa4cb10c9f/docs/implementation/price-oracle.md)). The largest unknown is σ_δ, the volatility of the gap itself. It has never been measured, and every loss figure scales with it ([delta-vault.md](../docs/delta-vault.md)). The jumpy-gap case still beat the pair by about $2.5k per event, but sizing for real money has to wait for the repo's planned backtest.

## Build the backstop beside the immutable exchange in three phases

### Phase 0 needs only keeper changes and ships in days

Phase 0 changes no contracts. It migrates the testnet vaults to the October 5 exchange, sizes the keeper's `orderQuote` to TVL, adds levels within the 48-order cap, and moves the half-spread floor toward 30 bp where the fee edge allows. It also adds a just-in-time quote. When a user asks for a price, the keeper posts an exact-size vault order at the δ-curve price with about a 30-second expiry, and the user's IOC order, or their one-click delegate, names that order. Every on-chain vault bound still applies, from the band to the imbalance and order caps. The cost is a second transaction and one block of latency per trade. That conflicts with the project's rule against added latency, so this phase is a stopgap, not the end state.

### Phase 1 moves the curve on chain without a 14-day extension

Phase 1 builds a `DeltaCurvePool`: a Δ-vault whose pricing function runs on chain. It lives beside the exchange as an ordinary account, as the current vault does, so it needs no extension window ([ConditionalStocksAdmin.sol](../contracts/src/ConditionalStocksAdmin.sol)). δ̂ is clamped to ±maxGap and moves linearly with the pool's YES-minus-NO inventory share. Both branch mids follow the formulas above, times a small full-set skew, and because δ̂ is linear in inventory the cost of a trade has a closed form. The stock price s comes from a verified Chainlink Data Streams report or a keeper-signed attestation, at most about 10 seconds old, with the market open and the price within the band of the Chainlink Data Feed. The probability p comes from a signed relay of the Polymarket mid, clamped per minute. The pool halts on an 8-point jump in p and when Polymarket reaches 0 or 1.

Execution reuses the exchange's existing primitives. The user grants the pool a delegation once. Each trade then computes the price, places an exact-size pool maker order that expires in the next second, and calls `placeFor(owner, IOC)` naming that order, so the order is consumed or cancelled in the same transaction and never rests. The fill emits the exchange's normal `Trade` event, so per-market fees, maker rebates and referral accrual work unchanged. A router fills better-priced book orders first in the same transaction. Gas is estimated at 250–300k, against 85k for a single-maker fill today. Wallet-funded buys could instead go deposit, split and `transferClaim`, but that path bypasses `Trade`. The pool would then have to emit its own events, and the indexer would have to learn them first, because the replica halts on unknown events ([ledger.ts](../packages/evm-client/src/ledger.ts)). Per-market caps cannot change after creation, and testnet markets allow $10k per order and $100k per wallet ([create-markets.ts](../scripts/evm/create-markets.ts)), so new markets should be created with caps that fit the curve's sizes.

| Parameter | Setting |
|---|---|
| Impact (keeper bps of δ per 1% of inventory) | about 14 / 35 / 140 for $100k / $250k / $1M pools |
| Half-spread h | 30 bp, widening to 3× in the final day |
| Maximum gap | 8%, unless an auction sets the starting gap |
| Imbalance cap | about 5× a typical trade |
| Per-block notional cap | about 2× the target trade |
| Slope | starts 2× steeper and deepens with cumulative volume, in the spirit of liquidity-sensitive LMSR ([Othman thesis](https://www.csd.cmu.edu/sites/default/files/phd-thesis/CMU-CS-12-123.pdf)) |
| Decay of δ̂ toward its prior | never; it forgets real information, and traders can harvest it |
| Range of p quoted | roughly [5%, 95%], following Hanson's advice to scale conditional liquidity by p(1−p) ([Overcoming Bias](https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/futarchy-liquidity-details)) |

### Phase 2 adds an extension, opening auctions and hedging

Phase 2 is optional. A native `swapCurve` extension would cost about 100k gas and move ledger balances directly. It needs the 14-day activation and its own audit, because extensions run with the exchange's full storage authority. Each new market could open with a δ-auction. When caps bind, the pool could hedge inventory with spot stock on Uniswap, which holds about 99% of Robinhood Chain's stock-token DEX liquidity ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/uniswap-robinhood-chain-stock-token-volume-2/)), and hedge the event leg on Polymarket.

### About $100k per stock covers a $10k trade, seeded first by the treasury

| Target | Capital per stock (half in stock) |
|---|---|
| ≤ 1% all-in on a $10k trade at p ≈ 0.5 | about $100k |
| High-gap events, or p far from 0.5 | about $250k |
| ≤ 1% on $100k trades | about $1M |

Worst-path inventory per leg was about 500 shares in the base case and 2,083 with a jumpy gap ([S4](../research_notes/Conditional%20market%20liquidity%20bootstrapping/mechsim/s4_out.txt)). The treasury should seed the first pools; Overtime's treasury has been the sole LP of its oracle-priced pool since June 2026 ([Overtime docs](https://docs.overtime.io/learn-about-overtime/overtime-amm-and-liquidity-mechanics.md)). In-kind deposits can open after that. The natural LPs are stock holders, because a vault LP's P&L is fees plus ordinary stock exposure plus the Δ result ([delta-vault.md](../docs/delta-vault.md)). Expected LP profit is about **+$6.4k per 30-day event** on $150k/day of uninformed flow and about +$2.1k on $50k/day.

Professional makers stay optional. Maker rebates of up to half the taker fee already exist. If Probabl adds rewards, it should copy Polymarket's two-sided scoring, which pays one-sided quotes at a third ([Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-makers/liquidity-rewards)), and require presence on both the YES and NO books. It should reward resting depth rather than volume, since Kalshi is ending its volume program amid allegations of inflated volume ([The Block](https://theblock.co/news/business/2026-09-30-kalshi-ends-trader-incentive-program-417247)).

Retail can leave the order book without the book going away. The router lets retail users see a plain swap ("buy TSLA-if-YES for $1,000") that never touches a ladder. The book stays for professionals and for anyone who prices p differently from Polymarket, and keeping it costs nothing because the immutable exchange is already deployed. Legal review is still needed: the repo flags conditional stock tokens as likely security-based swaps ([future-improvements.md](../docs/future-improvements.md)), so treasury-funded market making on them needs counsel.

## Conclusion

The search for a self-bootstrapping mechanism ends somewhere unexpected: Probabl's information problem is cheap, and its inventory problem is not. Discovering δ to within 1–10% costs a δ-curve $4–$446 per market at target depth, and an opening auction with a handful of informed bidders brings that down to tens of dollars. Delivering fully backed claims at ≤1% slippage on $10k needs about $100k of stock and USDG per stock. The lever is therefore cheap, shareable capital, not curve tricks, points or token emissions: a treasury seed, stock holders depositing in kind, one pool across up to eight events, and later the repo's "event universes" that share USDC-if-event across stocks ([future-improvements.md](../docs/future-improvements.md)). Every precedent paid for its depth somewhere. pump.fun charged later buyers, Polymarket spent $9.3M on rewards, MetaDAO borrowed half its spot pool, and GnosisDAO granted $100k ([GIP-145](https://forum.gnosis.io/t/gip-145-should-gnosis-dao-run-a-9-month-advisory-futarchy-pilot-with-100k-temporary-liquidity/11816)). Probabl's cheapest payment is a bounded δ-risk budget on capital that holders would keep anyway.

The design also changes which questions come next. Because it turns liquidity risk into oracle risk and oracle risk into signer risk, two pieces of work decide whether it ships with real money: measuring σ_δ and the Polymarket-to-stock lead-lag, which is the backtest the repo already specifies, and hardening the p relay. If δ proves large and jumpy, or no p signer is acceptable, the fallback is clear. Use the s-anchored variant for small-gap scheduled events, and the oracle-free split-seeded pair with lagging TWAPs for the rest, at 2.5–30× the capital. A side effect is worth building toward: an on-chain δ̂ becomes a public, continuously updated estimate of how an event moves a stock, the "Δ as a data product" the repo hopes to offer, provided readers use its lagging average rather than its spot value.
