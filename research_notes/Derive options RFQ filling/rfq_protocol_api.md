# Derive (formerly Lyra V2) RFQ: protocol and API mechanics

Researched 2026-11-08. The primary sources are Derive's current docs (docs.derive.xyz, which describe the **v3** API), the legacy **v2** docs (legacy.docs.derive.xyz, for the Derive Chain era of 2024 to 2026) and the derivexyz GitHub repos. Each finding says whether it is **v3** or **v2**. Lyra V1 (the AMM era) had no RFQ venue of this kind and is covered only as history.

## Q1. RFQ endpoints, parameters and lifecycle

### Takeaway
An RFQ on Derive is an **unsigned, two-sided request** for a package of 1 to N legs, sent with `private/send_rfq`. Approved makers find it with `private/poll_rfqs` or the `{wallet}.rfqs` channel and answer with **EIP-712-signed** `private/send_quote` messages. The taker picks a quote (`private/poll_quotes`, `private/rfq_get_best_quote` or the `{subaccount_id}.best.quotes` channel) and **signs** `private/execute_quote`, which settles all legs atomically at one `total_cost`. Every RFQ method is `private/*` and works over both WebSocket and HTTP POST.

### Cited findings

**Version context**
- The current docs describe "Derive v3", a re-architecture in which "The Derive Exchange and the Derive Chain is now combined into a single Zero Knowledge application that settles directly to the Ethereum L1". Users sign from EOAs or multisigs, with no smart-contract "Derive Wallet". The docs add that "The v2 -> v3 migration (more information coming soon) will automatically move ownership of user subaccounts…" — [v3 DevEx improvements](https://docs.derive.xyz/migrating/v3-improvements)
- v3 endpoints: mainnet `wss://api.derive.xyz/v3/ws` and `https://api.derive.xyz/v3`, testnet `testnet.api.derive.xyz/v3`. Margining, settlement and permissioning are "compiled into a RISC-V binary, and proven off-chain with the SP1 Hypercube zkVM". Batches are "submitted to Ethereum L1, where each batch is verified with a PLONK SNARK proof" — [v3 Introduction](https://docs.derive.xyz/getting-started/introduction)
- The v3 changelog has dated entries from July 22, 2026 to October 29, 2026. Examples are an Oct 7, 2026 entry adding `private/get_maker_rfq_history` and an Oct 13, 2026 change to MMP freezes — [v3 Changelog](https://docs.derive.xyz/changelog)

**Roles and overall flow (v3)**
- "The RFQ (request-for-quote) workflow lets a **taker** request a price on a package of one or more instruments and execute it as a single atomic block trade against a **maker**'s quote. It is the venue for large or multi-leg structures that would be hard to fill on the public orderbook." — [v3 RFQ Trading guide](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq)
- Taker flow, quoted from the guide: (1) `private/send_rfq`, leaving `counterparties` empty "to open the RFQ to all makers, or list wallet addresses to direct it privately". (2) Collect quotes with `private/poll_quotes` or the channels `{subaccount_id}.quotes` / `{subaccount_id}.best.quotes`, and track RFQ status on `{wallet}.rfqs`. (3) "Call `private/rfq_get_best_quote` with your legs and direction to get the best executable quote". (4) "Sign and submit `private/execute_quote` with the chosen `quote_id`. The engine re-reads the RFQ and maker quote and settles the block atomically." — [v3 RFQ Trading guide](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq)
- Maker flow: discover with `private/poll_rfqs` ("open-to-all, or those naming your wallet in `counterparties`") or the `{wallet}.rfqs` channel. Quote with a signed `private/send_quote` carrying priced legs, a `direction` and `max_fee`, and optionally `mmp`. Update with `private/replace_quote` (pass one of `quote_id_to_cancel` or `nonce_to_cancel`), or withdraw with `private/cancel_quote` / `private/cancel_batch_quotes` — [v3 RFQ Trading guide](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq)
- Signing split: "The RFQ **request itself is an unsigned intent** — it does not move funds… The maker signs each **quote** (`private/send_quote`, `private/replace_quote`). The taker signs the **execution** (`private/execute_quote`)." The signatures carry `signer`, `signature`, `nonce`, `signature_expiry_sec` and `max_fee` — [v3 RFQ Trading guide](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq)
- Scopes: each RFQ method needs `trade:rfq:<asset>` for **every** asset type its legs touch (`trade:rfq:option`, `trade:rfq:perp`, `trade:rfq:spot` or `trade:rfq:all`). Cancel methods need only one RFQ trade scope — [v3 RFQ Trading guide](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq); [Access scopes](https://docs.derive.xyz/authentication/access-scopes)

**`private/send_rfq` (taker, unsigned)**
- Request: `subaccount_id` (required) and `legs` (required, an array of `{instrument_name, amount, direction}`). Optional fields: `counterparties` (array of addresses, default null), `label` (default ""), `max_total_cost` (default null), `min_total_cost` (default null), `partial_fill_step` (default `"1"`), `client`, `extra_fee` (default "0") and `referral_code` — [v3 private/send_rfq](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatesend_rfq)
- Leg `direction` is "`buy` or `sell` — the taker's side of this leg". "Multi-leg RFQs let you price spreads, straddles, and delta-hedged option structures as one package that fills all-or-nothing at a single `total_cost`." — [v3 RFQ Trading guide](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq)
- Response (`Rfq`): `rfq_id` (UUID), `status` (`open|filled|cancelled|expired`), `valid_until` (ms), `creation_timestamp`, `last_update_timestamp`, `legs`, `filled_pct`, `filled_direction`, `total_cost`, `bid_total_cost`, `ask_total_cost`, `mark_total_cost`, `max_total_cost`, `min_total_cost`, `partial_fill_step`, `counterparties`, `cancel_reason`, `label`, `wallet` and `subaccount_id` — [v3 private/send_rfq](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatesend_rfq)
- v2 meanings of the cost fields: `bid_total_cost` is the "Bid total cost for the RFQ implied from orderbook (as `buy`)", `ask_total_cost` is the "Ask total cost for the RFQ implied from orderbook (as `sell`)", and `mark_total_cost` is the "Mark total cost for the RFQ (assuming `buy` direction)". v2 described send_rfq as: "Requests two-sided quotes from participating market makers." — [v2 Send Rfq](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-send-rfq)
- `cancel_reason` enum (shared by RFQs and quotes): `user_request`, `insufficient_margin`, `signed_max_fee_too_low`, `mmp_trigger`, `cancel_on_disconnect`, `session_key_deregistered`, `subaccount_withdrawn`, `rfq_no_longer_open`, `compliance`, `validation_failed` — [v3 private/send_rfq](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatesend_rfq)

**Cost limits (`max_total_cost` / `min_total_cost`) (v3)**
- "`max_total_cost` and `min_total_cost` filter the best quote. They do not limit execution. Total cost is in USD for the full RFQ size, from the taker's side. A positive cost means you pay. A negative cost means you receive. A quote for part of the RFQ is scaled to the full size." `max_total_cost` applies when the taker executes as buyer and `min_total_cost` when the taker executes as seller. Both use the same sign, so `"min_total_cost": "-180"` hides quotes that pay you less than 180 — [v3 RFQ Trading guide](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq)
- "Only `private/rfq_get_best_quote` and the `{subaccount_id}.best.quotes` channel apply these limits. `private/poll_quotes` returns every quote, and `private/execute_quote` fills any valid quote… Makers do not see either limit." — [v3 RFQ Trading guide](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq)
- The v2 description used a different sign convention for `min_total_cost`: "Positive values mean the RFQ sender expects to receive $". v2 also said "This field is not disclosed to the market makers." — [v2 Send Rfq](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-send-rfq). The v3 docs now say both limits use the same sign, where positive means you pay ([v3 private/send_rfq](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatesend_rfq)). The convention changed between versions.

**v2-only RFQ fields**
- v2 `send_rfq` also had `preferred_direction` ("If disclosed, the direction the user is aiming to execute as. Default None.") and returned `reducing_direction` ("If applicable, the direction from user's perspective that would reduce their position in each leg.") — [v2 Send Rfq](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-send-rfq); [v2 Poll Rfqs](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-poll-rfqs)
- Neither field name appears anywhere in the v3 OpenAPI spec (0 matches) — [v3 openapi.json](https://docs.derive.xyz/openapi.json)

**`private/send_quote` / `private/replace_quote` (maker, signed)**
- `send_quote` requires `rfq_id`, `subaccount_id`, `direction`, `legs` (priced: `{instrument_name, amount, direction, price}`), `max_fee`, `nonce`, `signature`, `signature_expiry_sec` and `signer`. Optional: `mmp` (default false), `label`, `client`, `extra_fee` and `referral_code`. "The quote's signature is verified and validated against the referenced RFQ before it is stored." — [v3 private/send_quote](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatesend_quote)
- Quote response fields: `quote_id`, `rfq_id`, `status`, `fill_pct`, `fee`, `max_fee`, `extra_fee`, `legs`, `legs_hash`, `liquidity_role` (`maker|taker`), `mmp`, `is_transfer`, `nonce`, `signature_expiry_sec`, `cancel_reason`, and `batch_status` / `tx_hash` (executed quotes only) — [v3 private/send_quote](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatesend_quote)
- Quote direction (v2): "`buy` means trading each leg at its direction, `sell` means trading each leg in the opposite direction". "The legs supplied in the parameters must exactly match those in the RFQ." — [v2 Send Quote](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-send-quote); [v2 RFQ Quoting and Execution guide](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/rfq-quoting-and-execution)
- `replace_quote` "atomically cancels an existing quote and submits a new signed quote for the same RFQ in one operation", taking the quote to cancel as `quote_id_to_cancel` or `nonce_to_cancel` — [v3 private/replace_quote](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatereplace_quote)

**Discovery and polling**
- `private/poll_rfqs` (maker) lists "RFQs that are open to all makers or that name the maker's wallet as a counterparty". It "Returns public RFQ views without taker-private signing material". Filters: `rfq_id`, `status`, `rfq_subaccount_id`, `from_timestamp` / `to_timestamp`, `page` and `page_size` (default 20) — [v3 private/poll_rfqs](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatepoll_rfqs)
- The `PublicRfq` that makers see includes the taker's `wallet` and `subaccount_id`, `legs`, `partial_fill_step`, `valid_until`, `status`, `filled_pct`, `filled_direction` and `total_cost`. It also carries two reputation fields: `fill_rate` ("Lifetime taker fill rate… null until the wallet has enough lifetime RFQs") and `recent_fill_rate` ("Decayed-recent taker fill rate"). It has no `max_total_cost` / `min_total_cost` and no `counterparties` — [v3 private/poll_rfqs](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatepoll_rfqs)
- Since Oct 7, 2026, `poll_rfqs` "returns only open RFQs, including partially filled ones. In v2 it also returned filled, cancelled and expired RFQs. Use `private/get_maker_rfq_history` for those." `get_maker_rfq_history` lists RFQs a maker was eligible to quote, "including RFQs you did not quote", and defaults to the last 30 days — [v3 Changelog](https://docs.derive.xyz/changelog); [v3 private/get_maker_rfq_history](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privateget_maker_rfq_history)
- `private/poll_quotes` (taker) "lists the maker quotes received against RFQs owned by the subaccount… Returns public quote views (including the maker's wallet) without any signing material." — [v3 private/poll_quotes](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatepoll_quotes)
- `private/get_rfqs` and `private/get_quotes` return paginated, merged views that combine open records with the archived filled, cancelled and expired ones — [v3 private/get_rfqs](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privateget_rfqs); [v3 private/get_quotes](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privateget_quotes)

**Cancellation**
- `private/cancel_rfq` (`rfq_id`, `subaccount_id`) "cascade-cancels any quotes makers have submitted against it, notifying those makers". `private/cancel_batch_rfqs` takes optional filters `rfq_id`, `label` and `nonce`, applied together; with no filters it cancels all of the subaccount's open RFQs — [v3 private/cancel_rfq](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatecancel_rfq); [v3 private/cancel_batch_rfqs](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatecancel_batch_rfqs)
- `private/cancel_quote` requires `quote_id`, optionally narrowed by `rfq_id`, `label` or `nonce`. `private/cancel_batch_quotes` takes any combination of `quote_id`, `rfq_id`, `label` and `nonce`, and with no filters cancels all open quotes — [v3 private/cancel_quote](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatecancel_quote); [v3 private/cancel_batch_quotes](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatecancel_batch_quotes)

**Public helpers and channels**
- `public/send_quote_debug` and `public/execute_quote_debug` return the EIP-712 encoded payload and hashes so you can check a signature before sending. They do "not verify signatures, persist anything, or place a quote" — [v3 public/send_quote_debug](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/publicsend_quote_debug); [v3 public/execute_quote_debug](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/publicexecute_quote_debug)
- Channels: `{wallet}.rfqs` serves maker discovery and taker RFQ status. `{subaccount_id}.quotes` carries a maker's own quotes. `{subaccount_id}.best.quotes` carries the taker's best incoming quote, with the same payload as `rfq_get_best_quote`: `best_quote`, `estimated_total_cost`, `orderbook_total_cost`, `estimated_fee`, `suggested_max_fee`, pre/post initial margin, liquidation prices, `is_valid` and `invalid_reason` — [v3 RFQ Trading guide](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq); [Subaccount best quotes channel](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/channels/subaccountbestquotes); [Wallet rfqs channel](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/channels/walletrfqs)
- No public (unauthenticated) RFQ feed is documented. Every RFQ channel is "Private channel (requires authentication)", and the only `public/*` RFQ methods are the two debug helpers — [v3 docs index](https://docs.derive.xyz/llms.txt)

### Inferences
- The RFQ is a two-sided request: the legs fix the package, and the quote's `direction` fixes which side the maker takes. The taker's executing direction is hidden from makers in v3, since the optional `preferred_direction` disclosure from v2 is absent from the v3 spec. This is an inference from the field missing in the v3 schema; I found no migration note about it.
- The taker's identity is not anonymous to makers. Makers see the taker's wallet, subaccount and fill-rate statistics, and takers see each maker's wallet.

### Gaps
- I found no documentation of a "block trade" endpoint separate from RFQ. RFQ is the block-trade venue ("execute block trades" is the OpenAPI tag description).

## Q2. RFQ and quote lifetime / expiry

### Takeaway
**v3: an RFQ is quotable for 30 minutes from creation** (`valid_until`). The docs flag that the window "is deployment-specific". Maker-quote and taker-execute signatures must stay valid until at least **RFQ creation + 31 minutes**, and at most **now + 1 day**. A quote therefore has no shorter TTL of its own: it lives until it fills, is cancelled or replaced, or its RFQ closes. **v2 differed**: quote signatures had to be valid at least 310 s ahead, and a quote "will be considered expired" once 300 s remained before its signature expiry.

### Cited findings
- "An RFQ is quotable only for a bounded window after creation (`valid_until`). The default window is 30 minutes." The docs add: "The exact window is deployment-specific and may differ per environment; confirm it for your target environment." `valid_until` is the "Millisecond timestamp after which the RFQ expires and no longer accepts quotes." — [v3 RFQ Trading guide](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq)
- Signature expiry table: "RFQ quotes — maker quote and taker execute | [minimum] 60 seconds past the RFQ's expiry (an RFQ is valid for 30 minutes from creation) | [maximum] 1 day". Nonce window for RFQs: "± 1 hour", must increase: "No" — [v3 Action signing](https://docs.derive.xyz/authentication/action-signing)
- "`rfq_creation + 31 min ≤ signature_expiry_sec ≤ now + 1 day`. The lower bound is 31 minutes after RFQ creation (the RFQ's 30-minute validity window plus a 1-minute buffer). The upper bound is the current server time plus 1 day." — [v3 Migration guide (breaking changes)](https://docs.derive.xyz/migrating/breaking-changes)
- On expiry, RFQ `status` becomes `expired` (enum `open|filled|cancelled|expired`). Quoting or executing against a non-open RFQ fails with `11104 quote_rfq_not_open` ("RFQ expired, filled, or cancelled"). Quotes carry the cancel reason `rfq_no_longer_open` — [v3 private/send_rfq](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatesend_rfq); [v3 Error codes](https://docs.derive.xyz/error-codes)
- Takers can end an RFQ early with `cancel_rfq`, which cascade-cancels its quotes — [v3 private/cancel_rfq](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatecancel_rfq)
- v2 quote and execute `signature_expiry_sec`: "Expiry MUST be at least 310 seconds from now. Once time till signature expiry reaches 300 seconds, the quote will be considered expired. This buffer is meant to ensure the trade can settle on chain in case of a blockchain congestion." — [v2 Send Quote](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-send-quote); [v2 Execute Quote](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-execute-quote)
- The v2 JS guide's examples set quote and execute `signature_expiry_sec` to `now + 350` s — [v2 RFQ Quoting and Execution guide](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/rfq-quoting-and-execution)
- The rewards program's RFQ "Time Score" has bands for time to first quote of "≤ 2 seconds → 2", "≤ 30 seconds → 1" and "≥ 5 minutes → 0.5" — [Institutional Trading Rewards](https://docs.derive.xyz/integrators/programs/institutional-trading-rewards)
- For comparison, orderbook orders have an expiry of at least 10 s and at most 120 days, or 15 minutes for MMP orders. They leave the book 5 s before `signature_expiry_sec` — [v3 Action signing](https://docs.derive.xyz/authentication/action-signing); [Orderbook Trading](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/order-types)

### Inferences
- In v3 a maker cannot make a quote expire sooner by signing a short expiry, because the signature minimum is RFQ expiry + 60 s. Makers must manage staleness actively with `cancel_quote` / `replace_quote`, MMP or cancel-on-disconnect.
- The rewards bands (≤2 s scores best, ≥5 min still scores) suggest makers usually answer in seconds, but late quotes inside the 30-minute window are accepted.
- The 30-minute figure is the only explicit RFQ duration in the docs. I found no stated 5-minute RFQ window. The only "5 minutes" in the docs is the v2 300 s quote-expiry buffer.

### Gaps
- I could not find the v2 RFQ validity window (`valid_until` minus creation). The v2 docs describe `valid_until` only as "RFQ expiry timestamp".
- The docs do not say whether a taker can set a custom RFQ duration. `send_rfq` has no such field in v3.

## Q3. Who receives an RFQ, who can quote, and maker requirements

### Takeaway
The taker chooses the audience. With no `counterparties`, the RFQ goes to **all approved RFQ makers**. With `counterparties` (a list of wallet addresses), only those wallets see it. Quoting is **permissioned**: an account must have the RFQ-maker flag (`is_rfq_maker`), or quoting fails with `unauthorized_as_rfq_maker`. In v2 this flag was granted manually by Derive support. Makers also need a funded subaccount in the same risk universe as the taker, margin for the quote, and the `trade:rfq:*` scopes.

### Cited findings
- "Leave `counterparties` empty to open the RFQ to all makers, or list wallet addresses to direct it privately." — [v3 RFQ Trading guide](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq)
- v2 field description: "Optional list of market maker account addresses to request quotes from. If not supplied, all market makers who are approved as RFQ makers will be notified." — [v2 Send Rfq](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-send-rfq)
- v2 guide: "Note that market maker wallets must be approved by the support team in order to get access to the maker API." The example code adds: "account owner of the subaccount_id must be approved to act as RFQ maker" — [v2 RFQ Quoting and Execution guide](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/rfq-quoting-and-execution)
- v3 error codes: `14027 unauthorized_as_rfq_maker` ("Account is not an RFQ maker"), `11106 rfq_invalid_counterparty` ("Counterparty unauthorized or nonexistent"), `11111 rfq_disabled_for_account` ("RFQ functionality is disabled for this account") and `11113 rfq_too_many_counterparties` — [v3 Error codes](https://docs.derive.xyz/error-codes)
- `private/get_account` returns "cancel-on-disconnect and RFQ-maker flags", including the boolean `is_rfq_maker`. Its `fee_info` includes `rfq_maker_discount` and `rfq_taker_discount` — [v3 openapi.json](https://docs.derive.xyz/openapi.json)
- Risk universes: "a trade, RFQ, or liquidation is rejected if the two sides sit in different universes." — [Managers & Risk Universes](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/managers-and-risk-universes)
- Margin and fees: `insufficient_margin` and `signed_max_fee_too_low` are RFQ/quote cancel reasons — [v3 private/send_rfq](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatesend_rfq). `rfq_get_best_quote` returns `is_valid`, `pre_initial_margin` and `post_initial_margin` (v2: "`True` if RFQ is expected to pass margin requirements") — [v3 private/rfq_get_best_quote](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privaterfq_get_best_quote); [v2 Rfq Get Best Quote](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-rfq-get-best-quote)
- Session-key levels in v2: `send_rfq` needed "account". `send_quote` and `execute_quote` needed "admin". `poll_rfqs` needed "read_only". In v3 these are replaced by granular scopes: `trade:rfq:option|perp|spot|all` — [v2 docs index](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/llms.txt); [v3 Access scopes](https://docs.derive.xyz/authentication/access-scopes)
- Rewards eligibility (separate from the maker flag): "The program is open to any firm or individual completing the application and meeting program requirements. All applicants are subject to review and approval by the Exchange." Order-book obligations include "Minimum quote size: 500", "28-day notional trading volume: > $50M" and "Trading volume share: > 2%" — [Institutional Trading Rewards](https://docs.derive.xyz/integrators/programs/institutional-trading-rewards)
- Higher API limits for market makers (500+ TPS matching against 1 TPS for traders) come by applying through Derive support or the rewards form — [Rate Limits for Integrators](https://docs.derive.xyz/integrators/trading/rate-limits)
- Legal rules: the RFQ and Block Trading Rules (updated 9 May 2026) cover "RFQ, block, OTC, principal, streamed price, negotiated, or similar transactions". Makers must keep quotes "reasonably connected" to market conditions, and Derive may reject, cancel or adjust executions that are materially disconnected (Mistrade / Erroneous Transaction review). Derive may also restrict "RFQ functionality" — [Derive RFQ and Block Trading Rules](https://www.derive.xyz/rfq-and-block-trading-rules)

### Inferences
- "Open to all makers" means all accounts flagged as RFQ makers, not every account. The v2 docs say so explicitly, and the v3 `unauthorized_as_rfq_maker` error and `is_rfq_maker` flag imply the same gate. I found no self-serve way to become an RFQ maker in v3. Getting the flag probably still means contacting Derive.
- Takers appear to need no special approval beyond the `trade:rfq:*` scope and margin. No error or flag gates takers, apart from `rfq_disabled_for_account`, which reads as an account-level kill switch.

### Gaps
- The docs give no explicit v3 rule for getting `is_rfq_maker`, and no capital, KYC or volume minimum for RFQ makers. The rewards thresholds apply to the rewards program, not to RFQ access.
- It is not documented whether a counterparty in `counterparties` must already be an RFQ maker. `11106` suggests "unauthorized or nonexistent" counterparties are rejected.

## Q4. Quote ranking and execution: manual pick, partial fills, multi-leg, signatures, settlement, fees

### Takeaway
Execution is **taker-initiated and manual**. The venue never auto-executes. The taker must sign `private/execute_quote` for a specific `quote_id`, any valid quote, not necessarily the best. `rfq_get_best_quote` and `best.quotes` only *suggest* the best quote, filtered by the taker's hidden cost limits. Partial fills happen in multiples of `partial_fill_step`, and every partial fill of one RFQ must be in the same direction. Settlement is atomic across all legs plus cash and fees through the RFQ module. RFQ fees charge the **taker notional rate to both sides** plus a taker base fee, with large discounts on multi-leg packages.

### Cited findings

**Ranking and choosing**
- `rfq_get_best_quote` is a "Taker-side dry run… Returns the best available maker quote if one exists. It returns no quote when the best quote breaks the RFQ's max_total_cost or min_total_cost." It takes `direction` (default `buy`), `legs` and optional `rfq_id` ("If not provided, will return estimates based on mark prices"). It returns `best_quote`, `estimated_total_cost`, `orderbook_total_cost`, `estimated_fee`, `suggested_max_fee`, `filled_pct`, `is_valid`, `invalid_reason`, pre/post initial margin, liquidation prices and realized-PnL estimates — [v3 private/rfq_get_best_quote](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privaterfq_get_best_quote)
- v2 meaning of `orderbook_total_cost`: "Total cost of the RFQ if it were to be filled at current orderbook prices (same direction as the RFQ). If lower than `estimated_total_cost`, the user may want to use the orderbook instead of RFQs for this order. Will return null if any of the legs do not have orderbook data or enough liquidity for the full fill." — [v2 Rfq Get Best Quote](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-rfq-get-best-quote)
- v2 guide step 5: "Poll for the Quotes (market makers' replies to RFQs) and pick the best one". Step 6: "Sign an execute message for the selected quote" — [v2 RFQ Quoting and Execution guide](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/rfq-quoting-and-execution)
- "`private/execute_quote` fills any valid quote. Check the quote's cost before you execute." — [v3 RFQ Trading guide](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq)

**`private/execute_quote` (taker, signed)**
- Required: `rfq_id`, `quote_id`, `subaccount_id`, `direction`, `legs` (priced), `max_fee`, `nonce`, `signature`, `signature_expiry_sec` and `signer`. Optional: `enable_taker_protection` (default false), `label`, `client` and `referral_code`. "It does not check the RFQ's max_total_cost or min_total_cost. The signature is verified and the taker fill, maker quote, and RFQ are updated together in one atomic step." The response adds `rfq_filled_pct`: "Cumulative fraction of the RFQ filled after this execution" — [v3 private/execute_quote](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privateexecute_quote)
- `enable_taker_protection`: "If true, reject the execution with `QuoteTakerCostTooHigh` when the orderbook fills the same legs at a lower total cost. The check is skipped when the orderbook cannot fill every leg in full." The matching error is `11110 quote_taker_cost_too_high` ("Taker cost exceeded the orderbook execution price") — [v3 private/execute_quote](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privateexecute_quote); [v3 Error codes](https://docs.derive.xyz/error-codes)
- In v2, the taker's execute `direction` is the opposite of the quote's: `direction: quote.direction === 'buy' ? 'sell' : 'buy'` — [v2 RFQ Quoting and Execution guide](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/rfq-quoting-and-execution). The Python example says: "The received quote direction must be the opposite of the RFQ direction" — [v2-action-signing-python rfq_execute.py](https://github.com/derivexyz/v2-action-signing-python/blob/master/examples/rfq_execute.py)

**Partial fills**
- `partial_fill_step` is the "minimum fill increment, default `1`". "A quote for part of the RFQ is scaled to the full size" when cost limits are applied — [v3 RFQ Trading guide](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq)
- The v2 docs contradict themselves. One description says "Optional step size for partial fills. If not supplied, the RFQ will not support partial fills." The response field says "Step size for partial fills (default: 1)". Quote `fill_pct` is the "Percentage of the RFQ that this quote would fill, from 0 to 1." — [v2 Send Rfq](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-send-rfq); [v2 Send Quote](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-send-quote)
- Errors: `11108 rfq_partial_fill_pct_too_high` ("Fill exceeds the requested total size") and `11109 rfq_filled_direction_cannot_be_changed` ("A later fill would flip the direction") — [v3 Error codes](https://docs.derive.xyz/error-codes)
- Partially filled RFQs stay `open` and appear in `poll_rfqs` — [v3 Changelog](https://docs.derive.xyz/changelog)

**Multi-leg rules**
- `11100 leg_instruments_not_unique` (an instrument repeated across legs), `11103 quote_leg_mismatch_vs_rfq` ("Quote legs differ from RFQ legs"), `11112 rfq_too_many_legs` ("Leg count over the maximum") and `14028 cross_currency_rfq_not_supported` ("RFQ legs span multiple currencies") — [v3 Error codes](https://docs.derive.xyz/error-codes)
- v2: "legs MUST be sorted by instrument_name" — [v2 RFQ Quoting and Execution guide](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/rfq-quoting-and-execution)

**Maker price protection (price banding)**
- "For makers, RFQ price banding is in place instead [of taker protection]… all assets in a multi-leg RFQ share the same spot shock value, and options don't consider volatility shocks. To find the maximum total cost of an RFQ, we first determine the maximum cost of each leg, and then add up the costs together." RFQ spot shocks: ETH 1.5%, BTC 1.5%, SOL 2.5%, HYPE 3%, XAUT 1.5%, CC 2.5%, ADA 2.5%, VVV/LIT/PUMP 10%. A breach gives `11107 quote_maker_cost_too_high` ("Maker cost exceeded the price bandwidth") — [Price Banding](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/price-banding); [v3 Error codes](https://docs.derive.xyz/error-codes)

**Signatures and settlement**
- v3 signed actions use `Action(uint256 subaccountId,uint256 nonce,address module,bytes data,uint256 expiry,address owner,address signer)`. The RFQ module is `RFQ_MODULE 0x9371352CCef6f5b36EfDFE90942fFE622Ab77F1D`, which also handles position transfers ("position transfers book as RFQ trades"). Orders use `TRADE_MODULE 0xB8D20c2B7a1Ad2EE33Bc50eF10876eD3035b5e7b` — [v3 Action signing](https://docs.derive.xyz/authentication/action-signing). The OpenAPI notes: "the RFQ module signs two different layouts" — [v3 openapi.json](https://docs.derive.xyz/openapi.json)
- v2 signing: "Market makers sign `{'max_fee': uint, legs: EncodedLeg[]}`" and "Takers sign `{'max_fee': uint, legs_hash: bytes32}`". "The smart contract ensures that the two parties agreed on the leg amounts / prices etc. by hashing maker's array of legs and comparing it to the `legs_hash`." Each encoded leg is `(asset address, subId, price, signed amount)`, and the amount sign is the leg sign × the quote-direction sign — [v2 RFQ Quoting and Execution guide](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/rfq-quoting-and-execution)
- v2 contract `RfqModule.executeAction` requires exactly 2 actions. It invalidates both nonces, checks `maxFee` ≥ the charged fee on both sides, and reverts with `RFQM_InvalidTakerHash` unless `takerOrder.orderHash == keccak256(abi.encode(makerOrder.trades))`. It then submits one atomic transfer batch: every leg, one net cash transfer, the maker fee and the taker fee. For perp legs the cash moved is `(traded price − perp price) × amount` (`_getPerpDelta`) — [v2-matching RfqModule.sol](https://github.com/derivexyz/v2-matching/blob/master/src/modules/RfqModule.sol)
- v2 `Matching.verifyAndMatch` is `onlyTradeExecutor` and accepts only owner-whitelisted `allowedModules`. Only Derive's whitelisted executor submits the matched signed actions on-chain — [v2-matching Matching.sol](https://github.com/derivexyz/v2-matching/blob/master/src/Matching.sol)
- v3 executed quotes carry `batch_status` (`Batching → Executing → Da → Proving → Settling → Settled`, each with an `…Error` variant) and `tx_hash` — [v3 private/send_quote](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/rfq/privatesend_quote)

**Fees (v3; the v2 text is essentially identical)**
- "Trades conducted via RFQs are charged the taker notional fee rate on both counterparties, plus a base fee on the taker side. Multi-leg trades receive up to 100% discounts on the cheaper legs." Legs are grouped into long calls, long puts, short calls, short puts and perps. The most expensive group pays in full, the cheapest gets 100% off, the second and third cheapest get 50% off, and other groups get no discount. 2-leg option spreads pay nothing on the second leg. Option + perp hedges pay nothing on the cheaper leg — [Trading Fees](https://docs.derive.xyz/integrators/trading/trading-fees); [v2 Fees](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/fees-1)
- Box spreads (4 legs, same expiry) pay a "yield spread" fee of `notional × 0.5% × years_to_expiry` on both sides, plus a $0.5 taker base fee — [Trading Fees](https://docs.derive.xyz/integrators/trading/trading-fees)
- Orderbook fees for comparison. Perp: taker `$0.01 + 0.03% × notional`, maker `0.01% × notional`. Option: taker `$0.5 + min(0.03% × notional, 12.5% × premium)`, maker `min(0.01% × notional, 12.5% × premium)`. Spot: no fees — [Trading Fees](https://docs.derive.xyz/integrators/trading/trading-fees)
- `max_fee` units conflict. The v3 fees page says `max_fee` "on `private/order`, `private/replace`, and quote submission is a per-contract cap denominated in USDC" ([Trading Fees](https://docs.derive.xyz/integrators/trading/trading-fees)). The v2 send_quote and execute_quote docs say "Max fee ($ for the full trade). Request will be rejected if the supplied max fee is below the estimated fee for this trade." ([v2 Send Quote](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-send-quote)). The v3 OpenAPI field gives no unit.
- Builder fees: `extra_fee` on send_rfq, send_quote and execute_quote. In v2 it had to be "between 0.000001 and 1000 USDC" and needed a `referral_code` — [v2 Send Rfq](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-send-rfq)

**Maker risk controls**
- MMP: quotes sent with `mmp: true` count toward MMP limits. A freeze cancels open MMP quotes and rejects new ones (`mmp_frozen` 11015). Since Oct 13, 2026 a freeze "stops only MMP orders and quotes", and a freeze tripped by an orderbook fill also cancels open MMP quotes — [Market Maker Protection](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/market-maker-protection); [v3 Changelog](https://docs.derive.xyz/changelog)
- Cancel-on-disconnect: "RFQ quotes are cancelled" when the WebSocket connection drops — [Cancel on Disconnect](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/cancel-on-disconnect)
- Rate limits: `send_quote`, `replace_quote` and `cancel_quote` each have their own per-(wallet, method) budget, "25 TPS → 125 points / 5s window" (mainnet reference snapshot) — [Rate Limits](https://docs.derive.xyz/rate-limits). "All other requests count as `non-matching`" (5 TPS for traders) — [Rate Limits for Integrators](https://docs.derive.xyz/integrators/trading/rate-limits)

**Maker incentives tied to RFQ**
- Options RFQ DRV pool: 200,000 DRV per 28-day epoch (ETH/BTC 100k, HYPE 50k, Alts 50k). "Only filled RFQs are scored… every market maker that submitted a maker quote is scored, not only the one that filled." The filling maker gets a 1.2× boost and the others 0.5×. Price competitiveness is measured against the taker's "Max Cost" — [Institutional Trading Rewards](https://docs.derive.xyz/integrators/programs/institutional-trading-rewards)

### Inferences
- "Best quote" presumably means the lowest total cost to the taker for the requested `direction`, after the hidden cost-limit filter. The docs never define the ranking algorithm, for example tie-breaks or whether partial-size quotes rank against full-size ones.
- On a single-leg RFQ the maker pays the taker rate (0.03% of notional) instead of the orderbook maker rate (0.01%). RFQ is fee-favourable mainly for multi-leg packages.
- The taker can get "last look": it sees all quotes and may let the RFQ expire. The `fill_rate` and `recent_fill_rate` fields shown to makers exist to price this optionality.
- Rewards are scored against the taker's "Max Cost", but makers never see `max_total_cost` / `min_total_cost`. Makers cannot target that number, and RFQs sent without limits may score differently. The rewards page does not say how.

### Gaps
- No documented numeric maximum for legs (`rfq_too_many_legs`) or counterparties (`rfq_too_many_counterparties`). The OpenAPI schemas carry no `maxItems`.
- The docs do not explain how the taker's `execute_quote` legs set a partial-fill amount, for example whether the taker can take less than the quote's size or must match the quote's legs exactly. The v2 signing scheme (the taker signs the maker's `legs_hash`) suggests the taker executes the quote's legs as quoted.
- No auto-execute or "fill best automatically" option exists in the API, apart from SDK conveniences. The search results show a `derive-rs` wrapper named `execute_best_quote` (docs.rs), which I did not verify.

## Q5. How RFQ relates to the orderbook (CLOB)

### Takeaway
The orderbook and RFQ are **separate venues with separate signed modules** (`TRADE_MODULE` for orders, `RFQ_MODULE` for quotes). A `private/order` with `order_type: market` crosses resting orders immediately and cancels any remainder, bounded by a signed `limit_price`. The docs describe **no automatic routing** between the two venues, no "RFQ fallback" and no size above which RFQ is required. The only links are informational and protective: orderbook-implied costs on the RFQ, `orderbook_total_cost` in best-quote responses, and the opt-in `enable_taker_protection` check at execution.

### Cited findings
- Orders: "Every order is placed with `private/order`, a signed action against the `TRADE_MODULE`." `order_type`: `limit` "Rests on the book at `limit_price`", and `market` "Crosses the book immediately; any unfilled portion is cancelled rather than left resting." "`limit_price` is **required even for market orders**… it acts as a worst-acceptable price bound." `time_in_force`: `gtc` (default), `post_only`, `fok` and `ioc`. "`market`, `ioc`, and `fok` orders never leave a resting order in the book." Flags: `reject_post_only`, `reduce_only` and `mmp` — [Orderbook Trading](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/order-types)
- Orderbook price bands: "Maker and taker orders cannot cross outside of the minimum and maximum price range". Spot and perps use spot shocks, and options add vol shocks through Black76 — [Price Banding](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/price-banding)
- Matching algorithm (v2 doc): "Perpetuals are matched through regular FIFO". "Options are matched through the blend algorithm": a FIFO pass, then pro-rata, then a FIFO cleanup. ETH/BTC parameters: `pro_rata_fraction` 0.8/0.8, `fifo_min_allocation` 10/1 and `pro_rata_amount_step` 1/0.1 — [v2 Matching Algorithms](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/matching-algorithms)
- The v3 `Instrument` schema still has the required fields `pro_rata_fraction`, `fifo_min_allocation` and `pro_rata_amount_step`, plus `minimum_amount`, `maximum_amount`, `amount_step` and `tick_size` — [v3 public/get_instrument](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/market-data/publicget_instrument)
- Pre-trade comparison: the RFQ object returns `bid_total_cost` / `ask_total_cost` implied from the orderbook. `rfq_get_best_quote` returns `orderbook_total_cost`, and v2 suggested using the orderbook when it is cheaper — [v2 Send Rfq](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-send-rfq); [v2 Rfq Get Best Quote](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/reference/post_private-rfq-get-best-quote)
- At-execution protection: "Takers can choose to reject a quote that is worse than an equivalent orderbook quote. This protection is off by default: set `enable_taker_protection` to `true` on `private/execute_quote`." — [Price Banding](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/price-banding)
- Position transfers use the RFQ path. v3: "RFQ quotes; position transfers book as RFQ trades" — [v3 Action signing](https://docs.derive.xyz/authentication/action-signing). v2: "The transfer is executed as a an RFQ. A mock RFQ is first created from the taker parameters…" — [v2 docs index](https://legacy.docs.derive.xyz/llms.txt)
- Rate-limit classes differ. Orderbook order flow (`order`, `replace`, `cancel`…) uses the "matching" budget (1 TPS for traders, 500+ TPS for market makers). Quote methods have their own budgets. Other RFQ calls are non-matching — [Rate Limits](https://docs.derive.xyz/rate-limits); [Rate Limits for Integrators](https://docs.derive.xyz/integrators/trading/rate-limits)
- The RFQ and Block Trading Rules say RFQ and block transactions "may be subject to different execution, matching, risk, margin, pricing, confirmation" rules than orderbook transactions — [Derive RFQ and Block Trading Rules](https://www.derive.xyz/rfq-and-block-trading-rules)

### Inferences
- A market order on Derive fills instantly only against resting orderbook liquidity (maker limit orders). RFQ quotes are not resting liquidity and are never hit by orderbook orders, since quotes are tied to a specific `rfq_id` and signed under a different module.
- The v3 instrument schema keeps the FIFO/pro-rata blend fields, so the blend probably still applies in v3. The v3 parameter values are not documented in the pages I read and would come from `public/get_instrument`.
- Routing between the venues is the client's job. A smart client could compare `orderbook_total_cost` with the best quote, or use `enable_taker_protection`.

### Gaps
- No documented minimum RFQ size or notional, and no rule that large orders must use RFQ. I also found no documentation of the Derive web app automatically sending large orders to RFQ.
- It is undocumented whether RFQ legs must respect each instrument's `minimum_amount` / `amount_step`.

## Q6. Options vs perps differences and documented limits

### Takeaway
RFQ supports option, perp and spot legs (separate `trade:rfq:option|perp|spot` scopes). All legs must share one currency (underlying), and both counterparties must be in the same risk universe. Options and perps differ in RFQ fee grouping, price-band rules (no vol shock for options in RFQ), cash settlement of perp legs, orderbook matching (perps FIFO, options pro-rata blend) and rewards weighting (only option legs count). Numeric caps on legs, counterparties or notional are **not published**.

### Cited findings
- Scopes per asset type: `trade:rfq:option`, `trade:rfq:perp`, `trade:rfq:spot` and `trade:rfq:all` — [v3 RFQ Trading guide](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/rfq)
- One currency per RFQ: `14028 cross_currency_rfq_not_supported` ("RFQ legs span multiple currencies") — [v3 Error codes](https://docs.derive.xyz/error-codes). Same risk universe: [Managers & Risk Universes](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/managers-and-risk-universes)
- Fee grouping separates `long calls`, `long puts`, `short calls`, `short puts` and `perps`. A hedged option (option + perp) pays nothing on the cheaper leg — [Trading Fees](https://docs.derive.xyz/integrators/trading/trading-fees)
- In RFQ price bands, "all assets in a multi-leg RFQ share the same spot shock value, and options don't consider volatility shocks". The RFQ spot shock differs from the orderbook ones; ETH, for example, is 1.5% for RFQ against 2% for perps and 1.5% for options — [Price Banding](https://docs.derive.xyz/trading/price-banding)
- Perp legs settle cash as the difference from the perp mark (`_getPerpDelta`: "If perp price is $2000, and the limit order matched is trading at $2005, the delta is $5") — [v2-matching RfqModule.sol](https://github.com/derivexyz/v2-matching/blob/master/src/modules/RfqModule.sol)
- Rewards: "Non-option legs (perpetual or spot) carry no RFQ weight". Option legs expiring in ≤7 days weigh 5.0, longer ones 1.0 — [Institutional Trading Rewards](https://docs.derive.xyz/integrators/programs/institutional-trading-rewards)
- Errors confirm that maximums exist without stating values: `11112 rfq_too_many_legs` ("Leg count over the maximum") and `11113 rfq_too_many_counterparties` ("Counterparty count over the maximum") — [v3 Error codes](https://docs.derive.xyz/error-codes)
- Instrument sizing fields (`minimum_amount`, `maximum_amount`, `amount_step`) exist per instrument — [v3 public/get_instrument](https://docs.derive.xyz/api-reference/market-data/publicget_instrument)
- Historical: secondary sources describe Lyra V2 as the move from the V1 AMM to a CLOB with on-chain settlement ("transitioning from an automated market maker (AMM) based protocol to a gasless central limit order book system (CLOB)") — [Amberdata blog (secondary)](https://blog.amberdata.io/ad-derivatives-insights-lyra-v2-protocol). The RFQ described here belongs to the V2/V3 era and not to Lyra V1.

### Inferences
- A delta-hedged option package (option + perp of the same underlying) is the RFQ use case the fee schedule is built for. A cross-underlying package, such as an ETH option hedged with BTC perps, cannot be one RFQ.

### Gaps
- No published maximum leg count, maximum counterparty count, minimum RFQ notional or maximum RFQ notional. Open-interest caps per risk universe exist (changelog parameter updates), but I found no RFQ-specific cap.
- No documented difference in RFQ lifetime or quote rules between options and perps.
